# Stocked starter-yard composition — source verification, 2026-10-05

The creator's Unity screenshots showed an empty foreground, an isolated manual bench and a plain office. The previous layout deliberately kept a 34 × 24 m view clear and put eleven substantial lots near the far perimeter. Replacing those lots' contents could not make the first working routes feel stocked. This change brings recognizable stock toward the office, bench and receiving routes while retaining player-designed construction.

## Implemented

`CompactYardClutter.Describe` now supplies 53 deterministic pooled modules in 14 batching sectors: the retained 29 candidates plus 24 new working pockets. New pockets include five populated parts shelves, two freestanding tool/drawer racks, five open mixed-metal skips, three tyre stocks, two stripped shells, two washer lots, two cooling-stock cradles, two cable drums and an oil/cable pallet. These are scenery, never free inventory, machines or XP.

Office frontage now has shallow fuse/conduit, pinned work notices, a repaired side sheet and a counter fastener tray. These 26 boxes remain against the protected office or on the existing counter. The office-side parts shelf/tool rack/tyres are separate replaceable modules. Both counter approaches, the gate lane and the receiving services stay open.

The central machine-building floor between x=-3..8 and z=-8..10 remains free of the new raised pockets, with additional build space around it. Nearby optional scenery does not participate in construction validation: a saved machine, loose job/item or belt route plus 0.4 m clearance hides its overlapping module. A shelf overlap does not remove its neighbouring tool rack, tyre pile or bench stock. Visibility changes reuse the same roots/meshes and hide the corresponding collision. The saved yard layout and progression are unchanged by scenery.

Restored player positions receive the same conservative startup clearance once, including when a new or loaded yard reuses existing stock roots. Ordinary walking and terminal work exclude the current player from saved-object occupancy, so approaching the bench no longer removes its tool wall after a transaction. If removing an overlapping saved machine or loose item would restore a currently hidden module through the actual player capsule, that module stays hidden until the capsule clears its footprint. Already visible stock remains visible when approached. A cached pending flag uses the existing unpaused 0.2-second view schedule to restore deferred modules after walking away; unchanged player positions and yards without deferred stock do no extra clearance work. No separate stock Update or geometry reconstruction is added.

Three new original fixtures share the existing, unchanged PropAtlas and importer: `CompactPartsShelf` (1,600 triangles), `CompactMixedSkip` (632), `CompactWorkshopRack` (1,380). Earlier FBX/texture/material bytes and GUIDs are retained. Existing authored washers, stripped shells, reels, radiators, motors, compressors and the already documented Poly Haven CC0 tyre provide varied surrounding stock. No Asset Store download or paid pack is required.

The stock has 37 coarse optional player-collision volumes, all on IgnoreRaycast layer 2. No new Rigidbody, light, interaction target, inventory or per-object Update is introduced. Twelve new single-mesh fixtures cast sun shadows; the many small separate offcuts and contact ellipses retain their cheaper shadow state. Stock still uses regional static batching. A source triangle ceiling is a resource guard, not an FPS measurement.

## Executed in this environment

- Actual Blender export/reimport of the three new FBXs: finite vertices/normals, nondegenerate faces, metre bounds, UVs and one atlas material pass. The independent pack is 3,612 unique source triangles. `python3 Tests/audit-starter-stock.py` checks these recorded hashes and independent membership.
- The actual C# clutter helper, core fresh model and `CompactDressingOccupancy` run against controlled hierarchy/value APIs. All 24 new pockets are visible with the actual fresh bench, car, fridge, fixed services and starting player. Gate/counter approaches and the central building floor remain clear. No machines/cash/XP are added.
- Every transformed mesh bound fits its declared footprint under a translated and rotated yard. Audited stock totals 281 pre-batch renderer objects and 79,604 triangles. When all seven newer salvage/fixture sources are unavailable, their procedural/earlier-source fallbacks total 447 renderer objects and 63,844 triangles. The 82,000-triangle/460-renderer ceilings include room for the already optional private piston. These are not runtime draw-call measurements.
- Scoped hide/restore retains mesh identity and does not clear the neighbouring district. The 53 candidates/37 collider declarations and twelve fixture shadow casters pass source/value checks. Invalid or missing models leave no phantom instance and retain shared source ownership.
- Nine focused actual-source visibility checks pass with warnings-as-errors through `bash /workspace/tooling/scrapshift-adapters/run-dressing-visibility.sh /workspace/scrapgame`: all 53 fresh activity flags remain unchanged after actual terminal bench work at z=-5.5; restored spawns stay clear; removing saved equipment or a loose item cannot re-enable stock through the capsule; touching capsule edges stay protected; a clear diagonal corner can restore; and the actual dressing coordinator partial's cached polling restores deferred stock without another transaction. Mesh/component/root identities are retained. The runner uses controlled hierarchy/value declarations, not Unity rendering, native collision or lifecycle.
- Updated native `CompactClutterTests` compile against controlled NUnit/API declarations; native execution remains unrun. Tests cover fresh visibility, modular occupation, containment, imported source ownership, pooled hides/restores, collider bounds/controller collision and the source budgets.
- New native `CompactDressingVisibilityTests` supplies the same nine throwing visibility/coordinator cases. Its wrapper compiles against controlled NUnit/API declarations; Unity execution remains unrun. `CompactDressingVisibilityCases` deliberately does not match the pure-core `*Scenarios.cs` source glob because these cases use Unity objects.
