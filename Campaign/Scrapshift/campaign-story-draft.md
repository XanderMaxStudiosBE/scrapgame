# Scrapshift

**Start with your bare hands. Build a scrapyard that works for you.**

Scrapshift is a planned first-person 3D scrapyard simulator with a retro visual style. Salvage discarded objects, decide what is worth repairing, recover useful materials, and gradually turn a small yard into an automated recycling operation.

## The game

Every piece of junk presents a choice: restore it for resale, dismantle it for useful parts, or process it into materials. The aim is to make those choices hands-on and satisfying, from carrying an appliance to a workbench to switching on a machine you have repaired.

The visual direction combines chunky models, worn industrial surfaces, muted colors and warm workshop lighting. The campaign illustrations show the intended atmosphere; they are concept art, not gameplay screenshots.

## From hands to automation

**Salvage.** Start small: carry objects, dismantle scrap and sort materials by hand.

**Upgrade.** Invest earnings in tools and powered equipment that take over individual jobs.

**Automate.** Work toward connected processing machines, conveyors and sorting stations. Arrange the yard to keep materials moving while you focus on valuable repairs and the next improvement.

Automation is central to the design. Each upgrade should replace a task the player has already learned, making progress tangible while keeping repair and resale decisions useful.

## Current development stage

Scrapshift is currently in concept and early pre-production. The core idea, progression and initial visual direction have been defined. A playable prototype has not yet been demonstrated in this campaign.

The first prototype is planned around a compact loop: collect scrap wire, process it manually, sell the recovered material, and purchase a powered wire stripper. This will help test the transition from handwork to automation before expanding the game.

Unity is the chosen development engine. Features described here are design intentions and remain subject to prototyping and testing.

## Why Kickstarter

The purpose of this campaign is to fund production of Scrapshift: developing and testing the game systems, building its scrapyard environment and machinery, and creating the art, interface and audio needed to bring the experience together.

The funding target, cost breakdown, reward tiers and delivery estimates are still being prepared. They must be completed before this draft is launched.

## Development approach

First, test a small playable handwork-to-automation loop. Next, expand the salvage, repair and production systems around what proves enjoyable. Then improve usability, save-game reliability and performance as the yard becomes more complex.

## AI-assisted work and concept artwork

AI tools have been used to help develop campaign text and generate the current logo and illustrative campaign artwork. These illustrations communicate a proposed visual direction and are not evidence of completed game environments or functioning machinery. AI assistance is also intended for development tasks such as writing and reviewing code; resulting work will need testing and review.

The final use of AI-generated assets within the released game has not yet been decided. Campaign disclosures should be updated as those decisions are made.

## Risks and challenges

The game is at an early stage, so the scope and schedule need validation through a working prototype. The main technical challenges include reliable interactions with physical objects, saving a changing yard, and keeping automation efficient as the number of machines and materials grows.

Features may need to be simplified or revised after testing. Concept artwork may differ from the finished game. A production budget and delivery estimate have not yet been established, and this campaign is not ready for launch until those details are supported by a realistic plan.
